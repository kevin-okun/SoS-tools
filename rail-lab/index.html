The apex is where the rail is widest; the edge is how sharply it meets the flat bottom. Fourteen shape-and-lean combinations were simulated directly (green dots): four apex heights across the lean range, most densely on the low-apex rail. Values between them are interpolated; two held-out simulations differed from the interpolation by 2.9% and 0.5%. Edge and thickness effects come from separate deep-water simulations and are applied as directional adjustments. The model is a 2D slice at fixed depth and one speed (3.4 m/s). Percentage differences between rails transfer to other planing speeds better than absolute forces do. Rocker, outline, and 3D flow along the rail are not modeled.

Data & method. Forces from 51 free-surface RANS simulations (OpenFOAM interFoam, volume-of-fluid) of a 47 cm surfboard-rail cross-section held at set lean and depth in a 3.4 m/s stream. The setup was validated against the 2D planing-plate benchmark of Rezaei, Ghassemi & Noshadi (2015) and the pressure distribution of Kramer, Maki & Young (2013). Error bars combine each simulation’s measured scatter with repeat-run differences at a perturbed time step, widened 3% between simulated points and by half of any deep-water adjustment.
